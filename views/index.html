Thai Government Servers Hit by Sophisticated APT Campaign Spanning 14 Weeks

BY SIRIPORN KITTISAK Mar 04, 2026 7 MIN READ BREAKING
// FEATURED IMAGE · 16:7 RATIO

A state-sponsored threat actor assessed with high confidence to operate from a nation-state adversary has successfully breached multiple Thai Ministry networks in a coordinated intrusion campaign that security researchers say is among the most sophisticated ever observed targeting Thailand's public sector.

The National Cyber Security Agency (NCSA) confirmed the breach on Wednesday morning, stating that at least three government agencies were compromised and that forensic analysis is ongoing. The intrusion, internally codenamed "Operation GoldenTeak" by responding analysts, is believed to have begun in mid-January 2026.

Initial access was gained through a spear-phishing campaign targeting mid-level civil servants, according to two sources familiar with the investigation who spoke on condition of anonymity. The lure emails impersonated official communications from the Digital Economy and Society Ministry.

Tactics, Techniques & Procedures

Once inside, the threat actor deployed a previously undocumented implant researchers are calling "BambooRAT." The malware uses encrypted DNS-over-HTTPS channels for command and control, making detection with traditional network monitoring tools extremely difficult.

"This is a patient, well-resourced adversary. The dwell time of 14 weeks before discovery is a serious indicator of their operational sophistication." — Anonymous NCSA Senior Analyst

The attackers conducted extensive lateral movement using living-off-the-land techniques, leveraging native Windows tools to avoid triggering security alerts. Data exfiltration was conducted in small encrypted batches over a six-week window.

What Was Taken

NCSA has not disclosed the specific nature of the exfiltrated data, citing national security concerns. However, sources indicate that personnel records and inter-ministerial communications are among the data categories believed to have been accessed.

Remediation efforts are underway and the affected systems have been isolated. The Thai government is working with international cybersecurity partners and has notified INTERPOL's Cybercrime Directorate.
